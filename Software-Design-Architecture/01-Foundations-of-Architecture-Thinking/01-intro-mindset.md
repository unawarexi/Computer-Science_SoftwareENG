# 01 - Introduction & Mindset

Welcome to your very first day of Software Design and Architecture! Today, we focus on shifting your perspective from merely writing code that works, to designing systems that endure. 

## What is Software Architecture?

At its core, **Software Architecture** is about the foundational structure of a software system. It involves the high-level decisions and choices that are difficult to change later. 

If coding is like laying bricks, architecture is the blueprint of the house. It dictates:
- How different components of the system communicate with each other.
- Where data is stored and how it flows.
- How the system can scale to handle more users.
- How secure and resilient the system is.

While a developer focuses on *how* to implement a feature, an architect focuses on *where* that feature belongs and *why*.

## The Architect's Role in a Team

An architect is not just a senior developer who writes better code. The role involves a significant mindset shift:

1. **Big Picture Thinking**: You must see the forest, not just the trees. You are responsible for the overall health, performance, and scalability of the system.
2. **Trade-off Analysis**: Architecture is rarely about "right" or "wrong" answers. It's about evaluating trade-offs (e.g., speed vs. cost, security vs. usability, consistency vs. availability). Every decision has a consequence.
3. **Communication and Leadership**: A great architecture on paper is useless if the development team cannot understand or implement it. Architects must document decisions, mentor developers, and communicate with stakeholders (like product managers and clients).
4. **Risk Management**: Identifying potential technical risks early and designing strategies to mitigate them.
